Health and mortality

ClaimFigureSourceStatus and caveats
“Deaths of despair”Rising midlife mortality among white non-Hispanic Americans, 1999–2013, from drugs, alcohol, suicide, and liver diseaseCase & Deaton, PNAS 2015; Brookings 2017Documented The authors attribute the trend chiefly to cumulative disadvantage in the labor market and to health-care costs, with weakened family and religious structure as part of the mechanism. Opioids “added fuel.”
US life expectancy78.6 years in 2017, down from 78.7 in 2016NCHS Data Brief 328; Woolf & Schoomaker 2019Documented Initially reported as three consecutive annual declines (2015–17); revised data show 2016 flat.
Religious attendance and deaths of despairWeekly vs never: HR 0.32 (women, n=66,492); HR 0.67 (men, n=43,141)Chen et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2020Correlational Prospective cohorts of health professionals; adjusted for many confounders but observational.
LonelinessDeclared an “epidemic of loneliness and isolation”US Surgeon General, 2023Documented A public-health advisory summarizing existing research, not a new study.
Burn-outIncluded in ICD-11 as an “occupational phenomenon,” not a medical conditionWHO, 2019Documented Defined by exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism about one’s job, and reduced professional efficacy.

Work, status, and self-worth

ClaimFigureSourceStatus and caveats
Busyness as statusAmericans read a busy life with little leisure as a sign of high status; the effect reverses in ItalyBellezza, Paharia & Keinan, JCR 2017Correlational Experimental and survey studies. The cross-cultural reversal supports a cultural rather than universal explanation.
Contingent self-worthSelf-esteem staked on external performance is associated with greater stress and instabilityCrocker & Wolfe 2001; Crocker & Park 2004Correlational A large literature; the essay uses it as the closest psychological analogue to the “minor premise.”
Protestants and working hoursNo effect of Protestantism on hourly wages; Protestants work longer hours, largely explained by valuesSpenkuch, JEBO 2017Contested Contemporary Germany, using the Peace of Augsburg as an instrument.
Farm share of US employmentRoughly 75–90% c. 1800; 1.6% in 2017USDA ERS Report 302 (2022); Lebergott’s historical estimatesDocumented Early figures are estimates and vary with how child and household labor is counted.

Durkheim’s religious gradientSuicide highest among Protestants, lower among Catholics, lowest among Jews (19th-century Europe)Durkheim, Suicide (1897); van Poppel & Day 1996; Becker & Woessmann 2018Contested Recording practices may inflate the gap. Amish physical activityMen 18,425 and women 14,196 steps/day; obesity 0% of men, 9% of womenBassett et al., MSSE 2004Documented One community of 98 adults in Ontario.
Amish suicideLower than general population (Pennsylvania, 1974–83); cases concentrated in a few families with affective disorderKraybill, Hostetler & Shaw 1986; Egeland & Sussex 1985Correlational Small numbers; genetic loading complicates cultural interpretation.
Amish retentionTypically 85% or more of youth join the churchYoung Center, Elizabethtown CollegeDocumented Varies by affiliation; likely lower in the 1960s.
Active Latter-day Saints, longevityLife expectancy at 25: about 84 (men) and 86 (women); mortality ratios 0.45 and 0.55 vs US whitesEnstrom & Breslow 2008Correlational The authors found similar ratios among comparably defined people of all religions, which points to lifestyle.
