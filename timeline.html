Predestination, uncertainty, and the search for signs of election.

“The elect bear fruit.”
c. 1730–1820

Enlightenment

The virtues kept, the dread dropped. Franklin as exemplar.

“The industrious prosper.”

Industrial capitalism; the self-made man; wealth as stewardship.

“The able rise.”

The iron cage named; new authorities compete: self, feeling, history.

Calvinist, c. 1550–1700

  • 1517Luther’s Ninety-five Theses. Ordinary work is soon recast as a vocation (Beruf).
  • 1536 / 1559Calvin, Institutes of the Christian Religion, first and final Latin editions.
  • 1630Massachusetts Bay Colony founded.
  • 1646Westminster Confession states double predestination for the English-speaking Reformed churches.
  • 1673Richard Baxter, A Christian Directory, Weber’s principal Puritan source.

Enlightenment, c. 1730–1820

  • 1728John Bartram, a Quaker-born farmer, begins the botanic garden near Philadelphia that survives today.
  • 1748Franklin, “Advice to a Young Tradesman”: “time is money.”
  • 1758Franklin, “The Way to Wealth.”
  • 1759Voltaire, Candide: “we must cultivate our garden.”
  • 1771–90Franklin composes his Autobiography, including the thirteen virtues.

Self-made, c. 1820–1920

  • 1835 / 1840Tocqueville, Democracy in America, on religion and voluntary associations.
  • 1859Frederick Douglass begins delivering his lecture “Self-Made Men.”
  • 1868Horatio Alger, Ragged Dick.
  • 1882Nietzsche, The Gay Science §125: “God is dead.”
  • 1889Carnegie, “Wealth” (“The Gospel of Wealth”). Nietzsche, Twilight of the Idols.
  • 1897Durkheim, Suicide.
  • 1904–05Weber publishes the Protestant Ethic essays (revised 1920).
  • 1912Durkheim, The Elementary Forms of Religious Life.
  • 1917 / 1919Weber, “Science as a Vocation” and “Politics as a Vocation.”

Therapeutic, c. 1920–1970

  • 1926Tawney, Religion and the Rise of Capitalism.
  • 1930Parsons’s translation renders stahlhartes Gehäuse as “iron cage.”
  • 1946Frankl, Man’s Search for Meaning (German original).
  • 1948Pieper, Leisure: The Basis of Culture (German). Tillich, The Shaking of the Foundations.
  • 1951Heschel, The Sabbath. Camus, The Rebel.
  • 1952Voegelin, The New Science of Politics.
  • 1957Rand, Atlas Shrugged. Tillich, Dynamics of Faith. Bellah, Tokugawa Religion. Samuelsson’s critique of Weber.
  • 1958Arendt, The Human Condition.
  • 1966Rieff, The Triumph of the Therapeutic.

Contemporary, c. 1970–

  • 1973Solzhenitsyn, The Gulag Archipelago. Berry, “Manifesto: The Mad Farmer Liberation Front.”
  • 1976Bell, The Cultural Contradictions of Capitalism.
  • 1978Havel, “The Power of the Powerless.”
  • 1979Lasch, The Culture of Narcissism.
  • 1981MacIntyre, After Virtue.
  • 1983Cowan, More Work for Mother.
  • 1984Borgmann, Technology and the Character of Contemporary Life.
  • 2007Taylor, A Secular Age.
  • 2009Becker & Woessmann, “Was Weber Wrong?”
  • 2010Han, Müdigkeitsgesellschaft (The Burnout Society, 2015).
  • 2015Case & Deaton’s first paper on rising midlife mortality. Cantoni tests Weber on German city growth.
  • 2019WHO includes burn-out in ICD-11 as an occupational phenomenon.
  • 2020Gallup: US religious membership falls below half. Case & Deaton, Deaths of Despair.
  • 2023US Surgeon General’s advisory on loneliness and isolation.
