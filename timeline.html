Events ↓

Reformation

  • 1517Luther’s Ninety-five Theses. Ordinary work is soon recast as a sacred calling (Beruf).
  • 1536Calvin publishes the first edition of the Institutes of the Christian Religion.
  • 1559Final Latin edition of the Institutes, with its full treatment of predestination.

Puritan world

  • 1630Massachusetts Bay Colony founded. John Winthrop’s “city upon a hill.”
  • 1646Westminster Confession states double predestination for the English-speaking Reformed world.
  • 1673Richard Baxter’s Christian Directory, Weber’s key source on work, time, and stewardship.

Enlightenment

  • 1732–58Franklin’s Poor Richard’s Almanack, ending with “The Way to Wealth.”
  • 1748Franklin, Advice to a Young Tradesman: “time is money.”
  • 1771Franklin begins his Autobiography, including the thirteen virtues.

Industrial capitalism

  • 1859Frederick Douglass first delivers “Self-Made Men.”
  • 1868Horatio Alger, Ragged Dick.
  • 1882Nietzsche, The Gay Science: “God is dead.”
  • 1889Carnegie, “The Gospel of Wealth.” Nietzsche, Twilight of the Idols.

Secularization

  • 1904–05Weber publishes the Protestant Ethic essays (revised 1920).
  • 1912Durkheim, The Elementary Forms of Religious Life.
  • 1917Weber, “Science as a Vocation”: disenchantment and warring gods.
  • 1926Tawney, Religion and the Rise of Capitalism.
  • 1930Parsons’s English translation coins “iron cage.”
  • 1952Voegelin, The New Science of Politics.
  • 1957Rand, Atlas Shrugged. Tillich, Dynamics of Faith. Samuelsson’s critique of Weber.
  • 1966Rieff, The Triumph of the Therapeutic.

Fragmentation

  • 1976Daniel Bell, The Cultural Contradictions of Capitalism.
  • 1979Christopher Lasch, The Culture of Narcissism.
  • 1981MacIntyre, After Virtue.
  • 1985Bellah et al., Habits of the Heart.
  • 2000Putnam, Bowling Alone.
  • 2007Charles Taylor, A Secular Age.
  • 2009Becker & Woessmann, “Was Weber Wrong?”
  • 2012Haidt, The Righteous Mind.
  • 2019–20Holland, Dominion; Henrich, The WEIRDest People in the World; Burton, Strange Rites.
