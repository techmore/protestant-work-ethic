THE QUESTION

Is it a saying or a theory?

Both. In everyday speech, “Protestant work ethic” means valuing hard work, discipline, and thrift. But the phrase has a specific origin. In 1904–05 the German sociologist Max Weber published the essays that became The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism. He argued that a particular religious psychology, mostly from Calvinism and English Puritanism, helped produce the mindset behind modern capitalism.

It isn’t originally American. Weber was writing about northwestern Europe. But Puritans carried the ethic to New England, and the United States absorbed it more thoroughly than almost anywhere else, which is why the phrase feels American.
