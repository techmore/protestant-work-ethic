HOW THIS STARTED

A question about a phrase

This began with a simple question: why do people say “Protestant work ethic”? The answer leads to Max Weber, Calvinist theology, and Benjamin Franklin. But following it carefully turns up a much bigger question underneath:

What happens to values when the story that justified them dies?

And underneath that, a more personal one:

Where do I look for proof that I’m okay, and is that a good place to look?

The work ethic is the clearest example we have of both, because you can trace it across four hundred years. It began as a religious answer to the second question. It outlived the religion. Its feelings are still around, mostly unexplained. If you’ve ever felt guilty for resting or measured your worth by your output, you’re in this story already.

  • Ways through
The Puritan wanted to work in a calling; we are forced to do so.
Max Weber, The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, ch. 5 (trans. Parsons, 1930)

Part I in one sentence. A behavior that began as a free religious response became, once it had built an economy, a requirement nobody chose. Part II asks what that feels like from the inside. Part III asks whether a calling can be recovered without the cage.
